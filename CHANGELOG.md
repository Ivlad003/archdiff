# Changelog

## Unreleased

The options of a picture, checked case by case on the page and fixed.

- A depth has no ceiling (the page stopped at 12): past 12 the picture is drawn with a warning that it may be big and slow to draw, first in its reason and on stderr for the CLI, and the depth field turns orange; a view that goes no depth, like the layers, says nothing.
- depth − and + step from the view's own depth (on a call tree, whose own depth is 3, they went to 2 and 0) and are off on a view that goes no depth; *full* goes one step deeper than the view by itself, as its tooltip said; the depth's placeholder is the view's own depth.
- One wrong value in a link (`limit=1.5`, `overlay=nope`, an old link's) reset every option of the picture, silently; it is left out alone now, the rest of the link kept, and the line under the picture names it. A fold of 1.5 typed in the field is a fold of 1 (the field wrote what its own table refused), and a field shows the value in force when it corrected one.
- *changes since base* outside git, or the *reviewer* preset there, broke the picture: it is drawn without the marks and says why.
- *reset* left the opened modules, the hidden lanes, type-only imports and *only the changed* on; it sets every option back. *remember as defaults* and *reset* say so after the reload they start (the notice was gone in a fifth of a second).
- Mermaid: the select lists the state diagram (a link with `mermaid=state` showed it blank) and greys out the types of another kind of view; a gitGraph or a state diagram asked for a picture it does not fit gives the picture's own type with a comment, not an empty block; `--mermaid flowchart` draws any picture as a flowchart (a machine's or a sequence's was ignored).
- A query's boolean reads `true`/`false` and `yes`/`no` beside `1`/`0`; anything else is refused by the API, and left out by the page with its name.
- The reasons count one shape, one storage, one state and one transition in the singular, and name a commit by seven characters.
- `archdiff web` says when it cannot follow the code. Node's recursive watch on Linux gives neither events nor an error once the system's file watchers are used up (ENOSPC), so the page silently stopped reloading; a plain watch of the root probes for it first, and the server's first line, `/api/views` (`watching`) and the stats in the page's header say why.

Switching between the pictures, walked on a browser extension's repository (scripts without functions, modules nothing imports) and fixed.

- A view opened while the first picture was still mounting the canvas left the canvas blank for good: a second Excalidraw was mounted into the same container. The canvas mounts once, and a picture that comes meanwhile waits for it. A picture slower than the next link (a big one, a slow machine) no longer lands over the picture the link names now: a load the link overtook draws nothing.
- The fit put a big picture's top rows under the toolbar and its corner under the minimap; it keeps clear of both (beside the minimap or above it, whichever is larger) and of the zoom row, and fits again when the window or a folded panel changes the canvas. The zoom and place of a view were never given back (the fit always won, and the place was kept every two seconds under whichever view the link named, often the next one): a view the reader zoomed or panned comes back where it was left, a place that no longer shows the picture gives way to the fit, `0` forgets it, and the places an older page kept are dropped.
- The whole map, `modules`, folded above 40 shapes like every other picture, so a repository of 48 modules showed 40 and three "… N in dir/" shapes; it keeps every module up to 200 (the fold of *full*), and the field *fold above* shows the view's own number. A picture bigger than the canvas even at Excalidraw's smallest zoom (the logic of a very long function, the classes of a big repository) was centred, showing its middle; it starts from its beginning, and the line under it says so.
- What lies beyond a picture's depth: each module and function of a picture that goes a depth knows its neighbours the picture leaves out — what imports or calls it from beyond (◂, before it, where its lines come from) and what it imports or calls there (▸, after it). The canvas counts them under the shape's corners (a tap on a count picks the shape), the strip of neighbours lists them as dashed chips that open the neighbour's picture (Shift: its focus view), the shape's page names them under *Beyond this picture*, and the picture's Markdown counts them beside each shape (`beyond` in the JSON).
- A picture with nothing to draw (no state machine, no cycle, a module without functions opened inside) was a blank canvas with a line of grey text at its foot, and the minimap still showed the last picture; the canvas says *Nothing to draw here* and why in its middle, and the minimap goes. A picture that could not be drawn (the timeline outside git, an unknown view) left the last picture on the canvas under no title; the canvas is cleared and says why.
- A module opened inside from any picture was empty when it declared no function or class — the common case of a script, an extension's page script, a build script — though its top level calls on: archdiff counts those calls as the module's own. The inside of such a module draws its top level and the functions it calls, and when the top level calls nothing archdiff followed it says so, with the count of calls it could not follow. A module picture of the module alone says that nothing of the repository imports it and it imports none of it (and how many packages `--packages` would draw); a call tree of the function alone says it calls no function of the repository.
- A second tap that opened nothing did nothing: on the function a call tree starts from (its own view is the picture), on an event, on a cell. The function opens its logic; a shape that opens nothing says so; Enter and the menu's *Open* do the same.
- A double tap could do nothing on a slower machine or a big file: the first tap's work (the file's code, the scene lit) kept the page busy, and the second tap was timed when it was handled, past the half second (a 2000-line module at a sixth of the speed). A second tap counts from its press to the first tap's release, by the events' own times.
- A tapped line's light did not go out: the hover and the tap each kept the line's look from before they lit it, so the hover leaving a lit line put back its unlit look, and the tap's light, cleared later, put back the hover's orange — which then stayed after a tap on nothing or on another line. Every element a light touches keeps the look it was drawn with, and its look is worked out from all the lights on it; a plain tap starts a new pick (the last selection goes, and a dim the dim tool does not keep); the editor puts every light out, as one would overwrite a colour drawn there.
- The project map scattered: every dot pushed every other however far, against a weak pull to the middle, so the modules nothing imports (an extension's scripts, a repository's build scripts) flew thousands of pixels off and the rest shrank to a blob — a map of 48 modules needed a zoom of 0.06 to fit and the fit stopped at 0.15, leaving 19 dots off the canvas. A dot now pushes only the dots within three ideal distances, every dot is pulled to the middle of the map, overlapping dots move apart, and the fit goes down to 0.05: the same map fits at 0.4, a folded map of 268 directories at 0.77.
- A double click on a dot of the project map never went inside: the first click had already closed the map.

Comparing branches, tested on a repository whose main moved on after the branches left it.

- `compare` measured every head from the tip of the base, so a branch "removed" everything the base gained after the branch left it: a module added on main showed as removed by every older branch, a tag behind main as a branch that deleted its work, a comment-only branch as one that removed a module. Each head is now measured from where it left the base — their merge-base, as its pull request would show it (the base itself when they share no commit) — and the table names that commit (*since*). A head named twice is compared once; an unknown ref is refused by name (`no branch, tag or commit "x"`), not with git's "fatal: bad revision".
- A module the heads touched in different ways read "~ every head" (one removed it, the other changed it); it is labelled head by head, "every head" only when they all did the same. Each head's section left out the functions it added or removed, its calls and its packages, so a head that only added a function had an empty section; it lists all a review lists, or says the head changed none of it. The lists of what every head and what one head touched missed a function removed. A module's page in a comparison said "changed in this change" under the checkout's own commits; it says what each head did to the module (and in a review, what the change did).
- A review of another branch (`review --head feature` on main, one head ticked on the page) started from the checkout's merge-base, main's tip, so it showed the branch removing what main gained since; it starts where that branch left main. The timeline, the history and the commits of the review's bar take the head's own start the same way.
- The review's title and Markdown named the base by its full forty characters; they name it by seven. "1 source files changed", "1 new layer dependencies" and "1 head add a layer dependency" read in the singular. The branch bar of a review of one branch ticked the working tree; it ticks the branch reviewed. `--help` gives the whole map's own fold beside the default 40.
- A line's label sat at the middle of its start and its end, which on an elbowed route is off the line: two heads' labels of a comparison read "featfeat-a" on each other, and in the flowcharts of a function's logic three labels in ten sat on a shape. A label now sits on its line — spots along each segment, the longest first, above or below, beside on the right or the left — clear of the shapes and of the labels placed before; where the gap is too narrow, the spot that covers the least. On a browser extension's 25 biggest flowcharts: 42 labels on another label and 196 on a shape before, 4 and 11 after. A click waits a third of a second for a second one. The `p` that opened the map was typed into its search box, which then faded every dot without a p.

## 0.5.0 — 2026-10-18

A contract the code is checked against, the business logic and the state machines of the code as pictures, a real run of an entry with its storages mocked, and a page that selects shapes, asks the agent about them and walks the whole project.

**Highlights.**

- The contract: `ARCHITECTURE.md`, one Mermaid flowchart, is the architecture the code is meant to have; `archdiff new` starts a project from it, `archdiff check` keeps the code to it, a review fails only on a layer dependency it does not allow, and the page's **code this** folds a drawing into it before the agent builds it.
- New pictures: `logic:` (a function's body as a flowchart, its paths as rules), `focus:` (everything joined to one shape), `machines` and `machine:` (state machines, written with literals or declared with XState, javascript-state-machine or Python's transitions), `metrics` (coupling and cohesion), `modules` (the whole map), cycles that say where to cut, sequences framed by their branches and loops.
- `archdiff run` and the Flow tab's **▶ run**: an entry runs for real under Node's permission model, its storages mocked, every function traced and set against the predicted flow; after every agent run the contract and the commands of `"checks"` run.
- The page: several shapes selected at once, a menu on every shape and line, questions to the agent about the picture or the selection, the project map and the minimap, the Spec tab, copy on every tab, the keys of every tool.
- One table of a picture's options for the CLI, the API and the page's link; every picture drawn one way for all of them; one vocabulary (`CONTEXT.md`) and four recorded decisions (`docs/adr/`).

**Incompatible with 0.4.0.**

- The JSON of a picture: `ref.id` is the ID of a declaration or a module only; a folded tail and a package say the view they open in `opens` (they carried a view key in `ref.id`), and a header of a matrix opens its layer or module.
- The API: `/api/node` and `/api/edge` are `/api/shape` and `/api/line`; `POST /api/layout` is `POST /api/drawing` and `GET /api/layout` is gone (`/api/scene` says whether a view is drawn by hand); `/api/review` and `/api/compare` are the views `review` and `compare` of `/api/scene`, `/api/diagram` and `/api/export`.
- The CLI: `archdiff layouts` is `archdiff drawings` (the old name still works); the `contract:` view is `usage:` (`contract:` is still read); `review` without `--base` measures from the merge-base with develop too.
- The page: the focus tool is **dim** (`d`, it was `f`), and the menu's *Focus* is a view of everything joined to the shape; the minimap's button says **minimap**; `/` focuses find and `v` the filter of the views; no letter key acts with Ctrl, Alt or Cmd held, nor in a select; the defaults a browser remembered with 0.4.0 are forgotten (they are kept in another form now).

**Fixed since 0.4.0.**

- A shape's page of a timeline, a review or a picture with `changes` was the shape of another picture (empty, or without the marks); a double tap on a module opened its dependencies while Enter opened its inside (both open the inside).
- The page reloaded only when a TypeScript or JavaScript file changed; any of the five languages reloads it. A `file:line` of Python, Rust or PHP in a page opens its code; a drawn label like `src/x.py` plans a module.
- An `archdiff.json` that is not JSON is named in the error. The help, the package description and the README named two languages and one runtime dependency (there are five and two).

**Simpler code, same pictures.** One table of the kinds of view (`view-keys.ts`) and one codec of what a shape stands for and opens (`shape-keys.ts`), shared by the exports and the page; the page in modules over its context (`app.ts` from 1380 lines to 146: the title, the panels, the picking, the menu, the canvas, the toolbar, the minimap, the options, the views, the exports, the keys); `serve`, the canvas mount, the TypeScript frontend's walk, the call resolver and the sequence view split into their parts; one look per kind of shape in the Excalidraw writer and its numbers named; only `git.ts` runs git. Exports, facts and graphs are byte-identical before and after on this repository, its fixtures and nine others. `CONTEXT.md` names each thing once (a drawing, a chain, dim and focus, a rule, a task, a link), `docs/adr/` records four decisions, and every module's header names its entry points. New tests: every wrong call, the exports as XML and as linked Excalidraw scenes, javascript-state-machine and Python's transitions, a folded sequence, an isolated module's metrics, a page reloaded for a Python file, the keys, dim; the test files no longer wait a minute for a stray timer.

**Everything new since 0.4.0**, newest first:

- The business logic of a function as a picture: `logic:<path>#<fn>` draws the body as a flowchart — a diamond per branch or loop with the condition as written (`if`, `switch`/`match` with an arrow per case, loops with `each`/`done`, `try` with `ok`/`error`), a box per run of statements, an ellipse per exit (a `throw` in orange) — and the Markdown lists every path from the start to an exit as a rule (the conditions taken, the calls made, how it ends); `logic` lists the functions with branches, the most branching first (every function now carries its count of branches). The body is read from the file on demand (`src/extract/logic.ts`), TypeScript, JavaScript and Python; Mermaid: `flowchart TB`; every export draws the new shapes.
- `focus:<path>[#<fn>]`: everything joined to one shape across the modules — a function's callers and callees (`--depth` steps each way), a module's functions with their callers and callees elsewhere and the modules joined by an import alone — each module a lane.
- The page: Shift+tap or Ctrl+tap selects several shapes (two light the chain of lines between them, more list themselves in a strip with *ask about them*, *dim others* and *clear*; the editor's own box selection counts too); a right click opens a menu on a shape (open, focus, logic, the other pictures, the selection, the agent, dim, code, copy), on a line (where it leads, follow) and on the canvas (ask about the picture, the project map, fit, clear, edit); the Chat tab's first row asks a question about the picture or the selected shapes, answered without edits (`@view:<key>` and `@logic:<path>#<fn>` tags carry the context; without an agent the question lands in the task box); the transcript folds an agent's tool calls and thoughts under one line and renders the answer; the minimap is on by default (remembered), draws the lines too and follows a drag; **project** (`p`) opens the project map — the whole repository as dots coloured by layer and sized by lines, laid out by forces, the current picture's modules ringed, hover lights the neighbours, a click opens the module, Shift+click its focus, a double click its inside, layer chips and a search box (`GET /api/graph`, folded to directories above 400 modules).

- `archdiff spec --tests` writes a test skeleton per Gherkin scenario (`tests/<feature>.test.ts`, each test to do with its steps as comments; a file archdiff did not write is kept), so the specification and the tests start from one list.
- Simpler code, same pictures: `buildGraph` is four passes (declare, bind, resolve, calls), `layout` is rank, order, grid and place (byte-identical output), `planFromScene` four steps; the page's tabs live in `web/src/chat.ts`, `flow-tab.ts`, `spec-tab.ts`, `review.ts` and `theme.ts` over one bound context (`page.ts`), `app.ts` down from 1900 to 1200 lines; `h()` builds elements; the editor has two typed conversions instead of six `as never`; the `Graph` is read-only; the fact cache is versioned by a hash of the extractors' sources. New tests: the extractors on snippets (TS, JS, Python, Rust, PHP), property tests of the fold, the intent's round trip, the layout and the grid router, the resolver, SCC, globs, the options table, the feedback arc set, the metrics.

- `archdiff run <calls:view>` and the Flow tab's **▶ run**: the entry runs for real, in a child Node process under the permission model (reads only, no child processes), every function of the repository traced at the start of its body, the storage packages (a database client, `node:fs`, `fetch`) replaced by recorders answering from the mocks (`--input`, `--storages`, or the mocks saved on the page). The report sets the functions entered, the storage calls and the result against the predicted flow: what it missed, what it did not foresee. TypeScript and JavaScript, a top-level exported function.
- After every agent run archdiff runs the contract check and the commands `archdiff.json` names in `"checks"` (`npm test`, …) and streams their results into the Chat tab (✔ / ✘ with the output's tail); **run checks** does it on request (`POST /api/checks`). Claude Code is allowed to run exactly those commands and archdiff itself (`--allowedTools`), so it can verify its own work.
- Lines around shapes placed by hand: when a saved drawing moved shapes out of the layout's columns, the lines are routed on a grid of the shapes' sides (A* with a cost per bend and per shared segment), so no line runs through a shape; a property test checks 150 random placements. A computed shape never sits on one placed by hand (it moves down its column until clear).
- Lanes as columns: `--lanes columns` (the toolbar's **lanes ↔**) lays the lanes as blocks across the picture, in the order the lines flow; a map of more than six lanes (`modules`, `layer:`, `dir:`) takes it by itself. The mix puts a class's methods in a lane of the class. The split pane stacks the two pictures when the window is too narrow for two desktop canvases.
- Symbolic links inside the repository are read (once, under their own path) when git does not list the files. Rust: `tests/*.rs`, `benches/*.rs` and `examples/*.rs` are crates of their own, so `mod util;` and `use <crate>::` resolve from them. Cursor's stream-json (`chat_id`, `--resume`) is covered by a fake agent in the tests; the CI workflow runs the GitHub Action on the repository's own pull requests.

- State machines: `machines` lists them and `machine:<path>#<name>` draws one (Mermaid: a stateDiagram-v2; Markdown: the states and a table of transitions with `file:line`). An implicit machine is a field or variable set to two or more literal states and read as one: its transitions are the assignments under a `case` or an `if` on the same field (`case "new": order.status = "paid"` is new → paid, in `pay`), an assignment under no such guard comes from any state, a union type naming the states declares the ones never set (dashed). An explicit machine is read from XState's `createMachine` / `Machine` (events, `after`, `always`, `invoke`, object and list targets), javascript-state-machine's `new StateMachine` and Python's `transitions.Machine` (with `add_transition`). TypeScript, JavaScript and Python (`self.state`, `if`, `match`).
- Control flow in the facts: every call records the branches, loops and handlers it sits under (`if`, `else`, loop, `case`, `try`, `catch`, ternary), read with tree-sitter queries; a `sequence:` view frames the calls under one guard as `opt` / `loop` fragments, nested up to three deep, in Mermaid, on the canvas and in the Markdown.

- Coupling and cohesion as numbers: `archdiff metrics` and the `metrics` view (a table; Mermaid: a quadrant chart of instability against abstractness) give every module and layer Martin's Ca, Ce, instability I = Ce/(Ca+Ce), abstractness A, the distance from the main sequence D = |A+I−1| (⚠ when far and coupled: the zone of pain or of uselessness) and the cohesion of a module's functions (one group by calls, shared callees and shared types: 1); `--overlay instability` shades any picture by I and marks the unstable modules others depend on.
- Cycles say where to cut: every import cycle (and, in `cycles:fns`, every circle of functions calling each other across modules) marks the fewest and lightest lines whose removal breaks it (an exact search up to 14 lines, a greedy order beyond), red and dashed with their weight; layers importing each other get a lane of their own; `--types` counts type-only imports too (dashed) in `cycles`, `cycle:` and `matrix`.
- A review with a contract: a new dependency between layers is a finding (exit 1, ⚠) only when ARCHITECTURE.md does not allow it; one it allows is reported as news. Without a contract, every new layer dependency is a finding, as before. Without `layers` in archdiff.json, the contract's layers place the files.
- A storage is known by what the code does: calls of the file system, `fetch`, the browser's stores, a database client's `query`, Python's `open`/`connect`, Rust's `std::fs`, PHP's `PDO`/`file_put_contents`, beside the packages and the names; `archdiff.json` `"storages": { "<glob>": "what it holds" }` and `"storagePackages"` name the rest.

- One description of a picture's options (`src/options.ts`): the CLI's flags, the API's query and the page's link read and write the same table, `--help` prints it, and a wrong value names its option (`--depth two`, `overlay=nope`). The CLI is a table of commands: an unknown flag is an error with a "did you mean" (`--dept` → `--depth`), as is one positional argument too many.
- The steps after a view (hidden lanes, the mix, hubs, the question, the fold, the weight, the review's marks, the overlays) are a list of pure steps (`src/pipeline.ts`) that `viewOf` runs; the CLI and the server no longer apply overlays and the review's marks each on their own; the timeline is a view like the others (its commits read at the edge, as every view's git and files are now, in `src/picture.ts`). A picture is drawn once per analysis and request (switching back and forth is a lookup). `archdiff.json` `"hubs": { "min", "share", "max" }` sets when a module is a hub. `archdiff intent --init --layers` writes the layers with a few modules each, which a repository over 60 modules gets by itself.
- archdiff keeps itself in its own contract: `archdiff.json` and `ARCHITECTURE.md` in the repository, `archdiff check` green; the matrix orders an acyclic layer graph topologically.

- TypeScript: an import type expression (`import("./x.ts").T`, `typeof import("./x.ts")`) no longer makes the whole file a syntax error (it is masked before parsing, positions kept); a dynamic import call is still a call. Found by running archdiff on itself.
- Monkey testing (random taps, keys, options, hashes; fuzzed API and CLI) found and fixed: a plain view got siblings like `module:modules`; the depth field took -1; a shape's page was built with the default options, so a function of a module opened in place had no page; the file route refused a package shape's package.json. `drawio.ts` and `excalidraw.ts` imported each other; the colour scale moved to `export/colour.ts`.

- The Spec tab: the repository's OpenSpec specs and Gherkin features listed and shown; *spec this view* and *review all specs* have the agent write or review them (the task goes through the Chat tab), *validate* runs the OpenSpec CLI; `archdiff spec [--prompt view|all|update] [--validate]` in the terminal. A **copy** button beside the tabs copies what the active tab shows.
- The specification: `openspec/specs/*/spec.md` (OpenSpec: requirements and scenarios for analysis, views, page, intent, agent, exports, review, cli; all validate) and `features/*.feature` (Gherkin, the scenarios the tests run).
- Simpler code, same behaviour: git plumbing in `git.ts`, shape builders in `nodes.ts`, the steps after a view in `shape.ts`, the server's routes in `routes/pictures.ts`, `routes/agent.ts`, `routes/git.ts` over a context; the page's state, navigation and graph helpers in their own modules. The duplicate view select beside the title is gone (the canvas toolbar has it).

- A hub is a module a quarter of the picture's modules import, or forty of them, whichever is less (a quarter of a 500-module map was never reached).
- The `modules` view: the whole map, every module in its layer and every import. A picked shape's neighbours as chips above its page (click: pick here; Shift+click: its own view), the arrow keys walk them (→ ← along the arrows, ↑ ↓ along the lane) and Enter goes in: sideways navigation beside the deeper one.
- A project from scratch: `archdiff new <dir> --lang ts|js --template blank|lib|cli|api|web` writes the skeleton, `archdiff.json`, `ARCHITECTURE.md` (the intent) and the first commit.
- The intent as the contract: `ARCHITECTURE.md` with one Mermaid flowchart (subgraph = layer, node = module path and description, arrow = import) that archdiff reads and writes; the `intent` view draws it against the code (planned dashed, beyond the contract orange); `archdiff check` fails on an import between layers the contract does not allow; `archdiff import <file>` takes a document's flowchart; `archdiff intent --init` seeds one from the code; `--view` folds a drawing into it; archdiff.json's layers follow it.
- The Chat tab: **code this** (the drawing into the contract, then the agent builds it), **improve the diagram**, **from document** (a pasted design note becomes the contract, through the agent when it has no flowchart), slash commands `/code`, `/diagram …`, `/doc`; every run carries the archdiff protocol; the agent's `archdiff-question` block shows as a question with option buttons, the answer resumes the session.

## 0.3.0 — 2026-10-17

(0.2.0 was committed but never published; its changes are here.)

- `web` without `--port` takes 7080 or the next free port above it, and says so; a named port that is taken is an error, as before.
- Eleven views that each answer one architectural question: `classes` (the class diagram, Mermaid classDiagram), `cycles`/`cycle:`, `matrix`, `type:`, `path:`, `contract:`, `events`, `tests`, `hotspots`, `packages`, `timeline`.
- Branches: a branch bar with the base (develop, main or master by default) and any number of heads; `compare` draws several heads against one base, every module labelled with what each head did; `archdiff branches` and `archdiff compare --heads a,b,WORKTREE` in the terminal.
- Flow: mocks generated from the types or by the agent from a description; ▶ play lights the steps on the diagram.
- Chat: `@module:` / `@fn:` / `@layer:` / `@dir:` / `@flow:` / `@type:` tags with suggestions, expanded into context for the agent; the shapes of files the agent touches light up while it works.
- The extractor records the event name of listeners (`on`, `subscribe`, `addEventListener`…) beside emitters.
- `--changes` (and the "changes" box on the page) lays the review's marks over any view: structure and change in one picture.
- `--mermaid c4|quadrant|pie|gitgraph|mindmap` writes the Mermaid type that fits a view; `classes` and `sequence:` have their own; the README says which fits where.
- Readability, after the research note (docs/research/readable-diagrams.md): views fold above 40 shapes (was 80); a module most of a picture imports becomes a badge "+ log.ts" on its importers (`--no-hubs` draws it); `--weight N` leaves out lines lighter than N; `matrix:<dir>` is the dependency structure matrix of a directory; a sequence keeps at most 12 lifelines and collapses a call repeated in a row into "×N"; the layout sweeps the ranks back and forth (barycentre) before placing; every picture names the question it answers and the commit it was drawn from; the page has reader presets (reviewer, newcomer, architect) and names the direction of the lines in its legend; a test keeps the crossings of the fixture's views at their baseline.
- A tap on a line lights it with its two ends and shows what it joins: the imports and calls behind it with their `file:line` (`/api/edge`). Several lines under one tap are listed to pick from; *follow* (Shift+tap) lights the chain onward and lists what it reaches; the line under the pointer lights on hover.
- Arrows never run through a shape: a route over several columns takes a corridor between the rows of the columns in between, a backward route runs through the gaps beside the columns, and a test keeps the invariant on every view of the fixture and on grids that force each case.
- Overlays over any view, in any mix: `--overlay tests,storages,heat` marks the modules no test imports, labels the storages and shades every shape by the calls into it; with `--changes` the review's marks lie over the same picture.
- The page's ⚙ options panel (`o`) sets the depth, the fold limit, the packages, the overlays and the Mermaid type; every option travels in the link, "remember as defaults" keeps them in the browser, "reset" forgets them.
- docs/media: screenshots of every view and a walkthrough video.
